## Migrated core behavior

- Isolated in-memory BFS/DFS frontier with URL deduplication.
- Independent page, request, byte, origin, frontier, URL, elapsed-time, link,
  and output limits.
- Exact-origin, domain/subdomain, segment-aware path, redirect, and downgrade
  scope policies enforced before every network hop.
- Per-origin start reservations, in-flight semaphores, robots delay, Retry-After,
  and adaptive 429 backoff on every robots, redirect, and retry attempt.
- One-hop redirects/retries, response byte limits, no-proxy requests, a shared
  validating resolver, DNS timeouts, and CIDR-based non-global SSRF rejection.
- IPv4-mapped IPv6 and RFC 6052 NAT64 normalization before SSRF classification.
- RFC 9309 robots access outcomes and matching, repeated-group merging,
  percent-encoding normalization, safe remote delay caps, and singleflight cache.
- Full-page `nofollow` and per-link `nofollow` handling.
- Structured outcomes/failures, crawler-owned analysis DTOs, streaming JSONL,
  bounded collected JSON, and stable automation exit semantics.

## Deliberate architectural change

Crawlberg performs fetch, link extraction, metadata extraction, and content
conversion inside one product model. `xcrawl` instead fetches once and passes a
`readabilities_rs::PageSnapshot` to `Reader::analyze_snapshot`.

The returned `PageAnalysis` contains full-page links independently from the
article result. Consequently a navigation/index page can return `no_content`
for its article while still advancing the crawl frontier.

The dependency direction is one-way: `xcrawl -> readabilities-rs`.
`readabilities-rs` has no knowledge of crawl queues, depth, concurrency,
robots.txt, or storage.
